## Accept / escalate rule

**Policy (conceptual — Figure 4 caption):**

> "Accept when confident, escalate when unsure. JEV produces a decision and a confidence signal derived from its label probabilities. Confidence controls the routing gate: confident decisions are accepted, while uncertain inputs are escalated to stronger LLMs, whose decision supplies the final verdict. For pairwise judgments, probabilities are aligned and averaged across both candidate orders before gating; the threshold is validated per workload."

**Single-order cascade (Section 7):** accept JEV’s decision when **`q ≥ τ`**, else take fallback judge’s decision on escalated items. Simulated **`τ ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.85, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99, 1}`** vs GPT-6 fallback (Figure 5; Table 13).

**Pairwise two-order gate (frozen policies, Eq. 1):** judge each pair in both orders; average aligned probability of semantic response A:

`p̄(A) = ½ [ p₁(A,B) + (1 − p₁(B,A)) ]`

**Frozen threshold selection:** chosen on **96 pilot selection pairs** (64 RewardBench + 32 JudgeBench) to maximize coverage while keeping selection-set accuracy within **two points** of fallback (Geifman & El-Yaniv selective classification); **invalid first-stage outputs always defer**. Grid/rule fixed before model expansion.

**Representative frozen policy (Table 3, extension 510 preference pairs, fallback GPT-6 Astra, τ=0.90):**

| Accept (%) | Cascade acc. (%) | Fallback acc. (%) | Δ (pp) 95% interval | Fee ratio |
|------------|------------------|-------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| 53.7 | 92.5 | 93.1 | −0.59 [−1.78, 0.59] | 0.568 / 0.622 |

## Headline accuracy and calibration numbers (verbatim quotes)

**Abstract — comparator gap + cascade:**

> "we find it within three percentage points of a state-of-the-art LLM judge, our strongest comparator, on ordinary preference and evidence-grounded factuality at 0.36% of the comparator’s fee."

> "A frozen cascade that accepts confident verdicts and escalates uncertain ones retains **99% of the comparator’s accuracy** at lower cost."

**Conclusion — cascade headline (names GPT-6, not GPT-4):**

> "accept when confident, escalate when unsure, and **keep 99% of GPT-6’s accuracy at 57% of its fee**."

**Pooled single-order cascade (τ=0.9, GPT-6 fallback, base order):**

> "At τ=0.9 the pooled cascade escalates 34% of items and scores 91.3% against GPT-6’s 91.7% (**99.6% retained**; paired difference −0.40 points, [−1.21, 0.31]) at 47% of GPT-6’s fee"

**Table 1 — JEV 1.13 base-order accuracy (%):** RewardBench **92.2**; JudgeBench **78.6**; HaluEval **87.5**; Existing labels **94.0**; validity **1312/1312**.

**vs GPT-6 on same table:** 93.5 / 93.1 / 86.7 / 96.7.

**Operating envelope summary (Table 2 guidance):** "Use JEV" on ordinary preference, HaluEval factuality, existing final-answer labels; **"Escalate"** on JudgeBench difficult correctness and RM-Bench style-adversarial pairs; **"Validate first"** on RewardBench 2 four-way and grounded summaries; **"Not supported"** on reference-free prose.

**Calibration / uncertainty (Section 6):**

- JEV Brier: **0.111**, **0.297**, **0.176** (RewardBench, JudgeBench, HaluEval); GPT-6: **0.104**, **0.095**, **0.245**.
- Error-detection AUROC (score `1−q`): JEV **0.869**, **0.745**, **0.863**; GPT-6 **0.891**, **0.907**, **0.899**.
- At `q ≥ 0.9`: JEV **10 errors in 199** HaluEval judgments vs GPT-6 **28 in 233** (coverages differ).
- Temperature scaling on extension: JEV NLL **improves** on HaluEval (0.333→0.284) but **worsens** on JudgeBench and RewardBench — **"No single temperature fits; each workload needs its own validation."**

**Reference-free prose boundary:**

> "Without a reference, all three are near chance and remain confident: mean maximum probabilities of 0.90, 0.95, and 0.96, a JEV Brier score of 0.815 with error-detection AUROC **0.518**"

## What “99% of GPT-4” refers to

**No claim in paper of “99% of GPT-4.”** The **99%** retention language refers to the **strongest tested LLM judge (GPT-6 Astra)** / abstract’s **“comparator”**:

- Abstract: **"99% of the comparator’s accuracy"**
- Conclusion: **"99% of GPT-6’s accuracy at 57% of its fee"**
- Section 7 pooled τ=0.9 simulation: **"99.6% retained"** vs GPT-6 alone.

**GPT-4.x** appears as **GPT-4.1 mini** and **GPT-4.1** baselines in Table 1 (e.g. JudgeBench 64.0% and 71.7%), not as the cascade retention denominator.

## Cost and latency (matched 120-judgment panel)

> "JEV’s median latency is **0.152 seconds** and its fee **$0.044 per 1,000 judgments**, against 0.548 seconds and $0.390 for GPT-4.1 mini and 1.885 seconds and **$12.182 for GPT-6**: about **9 and 277 times cheaper** on this workload."

Conclusion rounds: **"about $0.04 per 1,000 judgments at 0.15 seconds."**

Pooled τ=0.9 cascade to GPT-6: **~$6.3 per 1,000 judgments** at **47%** of GPT-6 fee (44% conservative bound).

Abstract fee ratio vs comparator on ordinary workloads: **"0.36% of the comparator’s fee."**

## Confidence: threshold transfer vs rank-only

**Used as escalation threshold (with local fit):**

- Frozen τ chosen on selection set; evaluated on held-out extension (Table 3).
- Checklist item (4): **"Treat confidence as an escalation signal, not a certificate; check it on style-adversarial pairs and validate any temperature per workload."**
- **"The rule does not always transfer"** — e.g. GPT-5.6 policy at τ=0.7 **loses 2.35 points** beyond two-point tolerance; some τ=0.5 policies collapse to order averaging with no fallback.
- Style-adversarial RM-Bench hard pairs: AUROC of `q` **0.770**; JEV wrong on **"a third of the pairs"** in `[0.9,0.95)` and **15%** in `[0.95,0.99)`; τ=0.9 cascade retains only **96.5%** of GPT-6 accuracy on those pairs.
- Reference-free prose: AUROC **0.518**, **"no threshold helps."**

**Ranking / error ordering (separate from calibrated probability):**

> "Error ranking and calibration are separate properties"

> "Confidence routes well where the first stage is competent but uncertain, and badly where it is confidently misled"

Figure 5 / text: accuracy rises monotonically with `q`; pooled AUROC of `q` vs JEV correctness **0.869 / 0.745 / 0.863** — supports **deferral signal**, not universal probability certificate.

## Limitations (verbatim / close quotes from §Limitations)

> "We evaluate **one proprietary JEV version** against a chosen set of configurations. **Training overlap and benchmark contamination are unknown.** Reasoning effort, model size, context limits, age, and serving systems all differ, so this is **not a compute-matched architectural comparison.**"

> "Decision-only prompts leave out generated explanations, explanation faithfulness, extended deliberation, and many grading rubrics."

> "The expansion followed inspection of the original study, and **every multi-family analysis is exploratory.**"

> "The human adjudication in Appendix I covers the **183 items selected by judge disagreement**, not a random audit of the benchmarks … **one human annotator** completed every item … **the adjudicator is an author** who had seen aggregate but not item-level results."

> "Simulated cascade fees say nothing about real cascade latency, and the **single-order cascades of Section 7 are post hoc**, their thresholds read off the items they are scored on; **only the frozen two-order policies test a pre-specified rule.**"

> "**Specialized professional domains are untested.**"

Ethical note: **"Confident errors argue against using any automated judge as the sole arbiter of consequential decisions."**
