## Task and pipeline (what was measured)

- Domain: Texas CRIS police crash **narratives** (2017–2025); quantity measured is **what the narrative states**, not ground-truth crash facts.
- Jev used as **System One** typed-decision model: fixed option sets, **no generated text**, probabilities per question.
- Two-stage population run:
  - Stage 1: **10-question** lean presence screen on **499,500** narratives (499,500 returned answers).
  - Stage 2: **27-question** full schema on **195,857** narratives (**150,000** random stratum for population estimates).
- Analyzed corpus denominator: **5,018,080** narratives (>40 chars).

**Abstract (headline scale + accuracy):**

> A screen covered 499,500 Texas narratives and 195,857 were coded with a 27-question schema.

> Against human labels the typed model attains an $F_{1}$ of 0.908.

> Recalibration on the same labels reduces calibration error by a factor of 3.3.

## One call vs batched questions

- **Full coding:** all **27** schema questions in **one call** per narrative (Figure 1; §3.2).
- **Screen:** **10** presence questions in **one call** per narrative (stage 1).
- Vendor interface (quoted, not adopted): returns all answers **"in a single parallel pass and with no generated text"** (§2.3).
- Efficiency claim (§4.1): **"asking more questions in one call is the dominant efficiency lever"** — cost dominated by question count $k$, not narrative length.

**Not one-call:** residual-identifier display screen is a **separate** single presence question (§3.1, Appendix A); not part of population 27-question coding.

## Human-label comparison (design, not verdict)

- Human reference = **what the narrative states** under the **same criteria text** sent to Jev; coders blind to model probability and coded fields (§3.3).
- Sampled by **probability cells** on model output; **calibration weights** (not simple inverse inclusion); effective info ≈ **1,265** judgments (design effect 1.95 on 2,416).
- **Coded CRIS fields** = separate large **agreement** reference (incomplete, one-sided omission); paper states agreement with coded fields **understates** narrative fidelity vs humans by median **0.26 kappa**.
- Narrative-only flags vs coded negatives: coders confirm **59%** [47–70%] overall on human reference (§4.4); splits by variable accuracy.

## Limitations and boundaries (§6 verbatim-heavy)

> The model is closed and commercial, its training data are undisclosed, and its behavior may change without notice.

> The pinned identifier and the recorded per-call identifier make such a change detectable but not preventable, so a reader cannot rerun the analysis if the vendor withdraws the model, and audits of commercial systems should be treated as perishable.

> The coded CRIS fields used as the large reference are incomplete, so every statistic computed against them is an agreement measure bounded by the reference’s own error.

> The accuracy claims rest on a human reference set that is smaller by three orders of magnitude and that is defined only on cases the coders found clear, because disputes were excluded rather than adjudicated.

> Its pairwise reliability is estimated on 94 triple-coded pairs, and its stratification on the model’s probability rather than on the positive class leaves the rarest variables with a handful of positive labels, as few as 5 for 2 of the 4 that fail.

> The weights … are calibration weights rather than known inclusion probabilities, because the frame was drawn by a greedy cell-filling rule and then screened.

> The recalibration maps were fitted on 3 coders’ judgment of Texas narratives and should be refitted rather than transported.

> The frontier arms ran with more context and more computation per judgment than the typed model, and their cost was not metered at all

> The analysis covers one state, narratives are written by officers with agency-specific conventions and are themselves incomplete records of the crash

**De-identification asymmetry (§3.1):** Jev population runs used owner-redacted text only; human coders and frontier models got **additional** regex redaction + identifier screen — agency should screen **before** first model call.

**Transfer (§5, stated as hypothesis):** schema/cost model may transfer; coded-field mapping and recalibration do not; accuracy couples **model + schema** (would fail on new Jev version without re-audit).

**§2.1 (schema sensitivity, not tested here):**

> Whether the option order or the criteria wording still moves the returned probabilities is a separate question, and this study does not test it

## Other load-bearing mechanics (for later phases)

- Output grid: probabilities on **two-decimal** grid (0.01–1.00); **resolution-floor** bound when grid floor > base rate (4 variables).
- Gating: dependent questions gated at $\tau_g = 0.5$; leakage rate $\lambda_v$ reported.
- Weak variables ($F_1 < 0.70$): unbelted, intentional, wrong_way, vehicle_defect — frontier arms recover 3/4 on same human labels (§4.3).
- Raw Jev not calibrated OOTB: Spiegelhalter **z = -5.3**, slope **1.63** vs prevalence; Platt → slope **0.97**, ECE **0.0069** OOF.
